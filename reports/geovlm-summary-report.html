TL;DRWhat v1 delivered

The research requirements asked a single strategic question: where can existing authoritative Earth-science information be turned into novel, application-driven geodata–language supervision? The framework answers it in three moves. First, a characterized literature corpus shows how the field builds geodata–language pairs and where those constructions stall. Second, the recurring strategies are abstracted into metapatterns and a formal Spec Card so candidate datasets can be specified before any data is built. Third, the patterns are run against live NASA and allied holdings, producing scored opportunities — and the top four are demonstrated with real constructed pairs, not proposals.

D1–D3The survey: how the field builds pairs, and where it stalls

Sixty logged arXiv queries yielded 448 candidates, screened to a focused corpus of 59 works (2021–2026) across all seven target domains (EO-general, agriculture, disasters, urban, mapping, infrastructure, defense/intelligence). Every record was retrieved live — nothing memory-cited — and characterized on controlled vocabularies: geodata type, language source, semantic relationship, alignment basis, pair-generation mechanism, scalability, capability-beyond-task, maturity, and four novelty dimensions. An LLM-assisted characterization was QC'd per protocol: a seeded 20% sample measured a 2.9% field error rate, one axis breached the 10% re-pass threshold and was re-verified across all records to a clean re-check.

The headline finding: the maturity cliff

30 of 59 works stop at benchmark research; only 5 reach a decision-support prototype; none reach operational deployment. All 16 works with no identifiable enabled use sit at benchmark maturity. The cliff is a construction choice, not fate: the works that climb it import an application evaluation together with the data — expert-written operational products, executable workflows, published standards — while pure semantics imports (web text, verbalized labels) stall on leaderboards.

Six anti-patterns distilled from the negative cases, each a "don't replicate" rule: template QA that answers itself; fluency mistaken for capability; assumed zero-shot transfer into expert domains; the imagery-to-statistics leap; LLM-synthesized supervision without hallucination control; and stopping at the benchmark.

D4Seven metapatterns and a Spec Card

Every construction in the corpus instantiates one of seven recurring strategies, each documented as the full ten-element chain (application need → … → application evaluation) with at least two corpus citations:

  • MP-1 Geo-registered knowledge verbalization — OSM/VGI joined to imagery by coordinates (SkyScript, RSTeller, LHRS-Bot).
  • MP-2 Label-to-language conversion — legacy annotations verbalized (RemoteCLIP, RS5M, ChatEarthNet).
  • MP-3 Expert interpretation harvesting — operational expert text paired with the data it interprets (WeatherQA, SynopticBench, AgMMU). The strongest NASA-relevant pattern.
  • MP-4 Structured observation rendering — surveys, event records, indicators rendered to language (LUCAS/AgriBench, ClimateIQA).
  • MP-5 Knowledge-bridged weak pairing — an observed entity bridges imagery to encyclopedic knowledge (EcoWikiRS).
  • MP-6 Workflow-grounded task synthesis — executable workflows and standards generate verifiable supervision (GeoDisaster, Recov-Vision). The shortest path past the maturity cliff.
  • MP-7 Constructed-truth synthesis — simulation and physics scaling where truth is known by construction (FloodLlama).

The model-independent GeoData–Language Pair Spec Card (16 mandatory fields including a named workflow owner, a negative-control plan, and metapattern lineage) was validated by back-filling two published datasets — SkyScript and WeatherQA — without needing any field the template lacked.

D5Opportunities in NASA and allied holdings

Twelve holdings were probed live — content samples inspected, no hypothetical sources: NASA CMR/Earthdata, EONET, GIBS/Worldview, granule metadata, the NASA Image Library, astronaut photography, Earth Observatory feeds, NWS forecast products, USGS earthquake products, GLOBE Observer, GBIF, and USDA NASS. The unexploited-by-the-corpus check confirmed that CMR collection summaries, USGS PAGER impact text, GLOBE citizen captions, NASA's narrative archives, and NASS crop-condition language have never been used as multimodal supervision in the surveyed literature. Crossing metapatterns × holdings × domains produced eleven candidates, each a complete Spec Card.

✓Verification and honest caveats

  • Tests: 55 pytest tests pass, covering corpus schema, characterization vocabularies, spec-card completeness, and byte-for-byte reproducibility of curation and score tables.
  • Numbers: every count in this report regenerates from analysis/ scripts over committed data.
  • Caveats: characterization is abstract-based (fields papers don't state are an explicit unknown — 14 records on pair-generation); demo geodata sides are authoritative product references, not downloaded rasters; the corpus is arXiv-only under documented inclusion rules, so journal-only GeoVLM work is not represented.

  • Obtain a free NASS API key to unlock the highest-application-value deferred candidate (crop-condition scenes).

  • Use the Spec Card as the standing intake format for any proposed geodata–language dataset — its mandatory workflow-owner and negative-control fields are what keep new work off the maturity cliff.
